# Travel Costing and Quotation Portfolio

Anonymised samples of how I cost, compare and quote bespoke trips. All client details, supplier net rates and margins have been removed. **Every figure is illustrative.**

**Workbook:** `Sample_Travel_Costing_Portfolio.xlsx` (Excel). Blue text = input, black = formula, yellow fill = key assumption.

---

## 1. Maldives Resort Comparison

**Brief:** A family of two adults and one child wants five nights in the Maldives in peak season, all-inclusive where possible, with speedboat transfers.

**Approach**
- Shortlisted six resorts and put package price (couple plus child), meal plan and room combination side by side.
- Added the lowest online price (Booking.com and MakeMyTrip) as a benchmark.
- Applied a markup per resort, then calculated the agency quote, the saving against the lowest online price, and a rank.
- Added a price check that flags any quote that is not competitive.

**Result:** A one-page view that shows the client which resort gives the best value, and shows me which quotes need re-pricing before they are sent.

**Skills shown:** supplier comparison, markup and margin logic, benchmarking, Excel (`IF`, `MIN`, `RANK`).

## 2. Bali Hotel Comparison

**Brief:** A family stay split between Ubud (3 nights) and Seminyak (4 nights), with a mix of luxury hotels and villas.

**Approach**
- Listed ten room options across six hotels with rooms required, nights and the rate per room for the full stay.
- Calculated the quoted total and compared it with the Booking.com total for the same rooms.
- Flagged rows where the online price is cheaper and rows with no online check.

**Result:** A clear shortlist of options where the quote beats online prices, and a visible list of rows to re-price or drop.

**Skills shown:** rate comparison, handling missing data, conditional flags, hotel and room-category knowledge.

## 3. Honeymoon Costing: Zanzibar (5N) + South Africa (11N)

**Brief:** A 16-night honeymoon combining a beach stay in Zanzibar with a self-drive through Cape Town, the Garden Route and the Winelands.

**Approach**
- Built the South Africa cost from the bottom up: four hotels (11 nights), car hire, activities, entrances and ground handling.
- Added an agency markup, then converted ZAR to INR and added 2% TCS.
- Priced four Zanzibar resort options in USD and showed a combined total for each, excluding flights.
- Wrote clear inclusions and exclusions so the client knows exactly what is and is not covered.

**Result:** One costing that moves from net supplier cost to the final INR price, with every assumption (markup, exchange rates, TCS) in its own cell so it can be changed in seconds.

**Skills shown:** multi-currency costing, markup logic, package building, inclusions and exclusions.

## 5. Scotland and London: Harry Potter Steam Train + West End Show

**Brief:** A couple wants a 9-day trip built around two bucket-list experiences: the Jacobite steam train (the "Hogwarts Express") over the Glenfinnan Viaduct, and an evening at a West End show in London.

**Approach**
- Booked the Jacobite first, because it sells out weeks ahead in peak season (mid-May to October), then built the route around it: London, Edinburgh, Fort William, Isle of Skye and out through Inverness.
- Used a train between London and Edinburgh and a hire car for the Highlands, with a one-way drop at Inverness and a short flight back to London.
- Costed from published and estimated prices: the 2026 Jacobite First Class fare and West End seat tiers from an industry price survey, with hotels, transfers and experiences at planning estimates.
- Added a "price basis" column so every line says whether it is a published fare or an estimate, and kept the West End seat price as an input so the client can choose a budget, mid-range or premium seat.

**Result:** An itinerary and approximate costing in GBP and INR (including markup and TCS), clear about what is confirmed and what must be re-checked before quoting.

**Skills shown:** itinerary design around a high-demand experience, multi-leg logistics (rail, road, air), costing with transparent assumptions, destination and experience knowledge.
